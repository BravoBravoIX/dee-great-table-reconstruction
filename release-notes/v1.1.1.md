# v1.1.1 — The Enochian Great Table, 1584 against 1587: working paper with code, data and results

Working paper (not peer reviewed), with its code, data and results. The paper is `paper/great-table.pdf` in this archive.

**What changed.** Documentation update: adds a "How AI was used" section and tidies wording. Results and data unchanged.

**Abstract.** John Dee's and Edward Kelley's "Great Table" of 1584, four lettered Watchtowers joined by a Black Cross, survives in a 1584 form and in a "reformed" form of 1587 that rearranges the quarters and changes 23 letters. Which letters the table should carry has been argued over by practitioners and historians, most recently in a careful manual comparison published in December 2025. This study weighs every surviving witness to each of the 624 cells at once (four manuscript witnesses read from published renderings, five secondary ones, the 91 governors' names read along their sigils, names the angels spoke aloud, Dee's later working names and the 1587 number cipher) in one constraint model with weights declared in advance, and checks every result against controls. The 1587 cipher was built on the reformed arrangement, numbered row by row (190 of 196 numbers hit, against at most 48 in 10,000 shuffles). Its well-known off-by-one slips are not scattered errors but one offset block over numbers 121–414 (8 switches between exact and slip, against at least 63 in every shuffle), which shows the cipher was worked on the reformed table with the shifted row of MOR. At all 26 disputed cells the evidence weighs toward the 1584 letters; with each name hidden in turn the rebuilt table predicts 71 of 87 names, against none for shuffled tables. Kelley's 1587 changes behave like random edits of the same shape, except the one that fixes the name Aydropt. A known-answer test shows that the 16 conflicts between the table and the governors' names cannot be decided from these documents.

**Results in brief**
- Arrangement: the 1587 cipher was built on ORO MPH / MOR OIP, numbered row by row: 190 of 196 numbers hit (105 exact), against a mean of 26.6 and a maximum of 48 in 10,000 shuffles. No other placement comes close.
- The cipher's slips form one offset block, numbers 121–414: 8 switches between exact and slip, against about 90 if scattered (never fewer than 63 in 10,000 trials). The cipher therefore fixes the shifted MOR row, *t* at ORO 7.0 and *p* at OIP 11.3 as the letters it was worked on.
- The 26 disputed cells: the 1584 letters at all 26 at the default weights; 24 keep it in at least 1,116 of 1,296 weightings. ORO 0.7 (u/y, Aydropt) is a tie.
- The rebuilt table carries real information: with each name hidden in turn it predicts 71 of 87 names exactly; shuffled tables predict none.
- Kelley's 1587 changes break names at the random rate (12 against 12.7, p ≈ 0.45); only the Aydropt fix stands out (p ≈ 0.012).
- The 16 governor-name conflicts cannot be decided from these documents (known-answer test); two lean to the table letter.

**Limits.** The manuscript witnesses were read from the Vovina Archives renderings, not from manuscript images. Dee's margin note on the off-by-one numbers (Klein's edition) and several print-only studies were not read; they may already contain points listed as new.

**Builds on** AZOTH, *Enochian Tools* (1993); Burns and Moore (2010); Frater Rinasce Più Gloriosa, "Reconstructing the True Great Table" (2025); the Vovina Archives; Casaubon (1659); Klein (2020).
